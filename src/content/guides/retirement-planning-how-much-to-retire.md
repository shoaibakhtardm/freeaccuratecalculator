## 1. The Core Retirement Formula: The 25x Rule

The most mathematically robust framework for calculating your target nest egg is derived from the **Trinity Study (Bengen, 1994)**, widely known as the **4% Safe Withdrawal Rate (SWR)**.

To calculate your target nest egg, multiply your expected annual retirement expenses by **25**:

$$\text{Target Retirement Corpus} = \text{Annual Retirement Expenses} \times 25$$

### Examples:
* **$40,000/year expenses:** Target = $40,000 \times 25 = \mathbf{\$1,000,000}$
* **$60,000/year expenses:** Target = $60,000 \times 25 = \mathbf{\$1,500,000}$
* **$100,000/year expenses:** Target = $100,000 \times 25 = \mathbf{\$2,500,000}$

*Why 25?* Because $1 \div 0.04 = 25$. If you withdraw 4% each year, your capital base can withstand historic market drops and maintain purchasing power over 30+ years.

## 2. Adjusting for Inflation: The Silent Wealth Destroyer

One of the most dangerous errors in retirement planning is failing to adjust future living expenses for inflation. 

At a 3.5% historical inflation rate, what costs $5,000 per month today will cost over **$11,800 per month in 25 years**!

### The Future Expense Equation:
$$\text{Future Expense} = \text{Current Expense} \times (1 + i)^t$$

Where $i$ is annual inflation and $t$ is the number of years until retirement.

| Years to Retirement | Current Monthly Need | Future Monthly Need (at 4% Inflation) | Target Corpus Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **10 Years** | $4,000 | $5,921 | $1,776,300 |
| **20 Years** | $4,000 | $8,765 | $2,629,500 |
| **30 Years** | $4,000 | $12,974 | $3,892,200 |

## 3. Sequence of Returns Risk (SRR)

The order of investment returns matters enormously once you transition from saving to withdrawing:
* Experiencing a severe market crash during your **first 3 to 5 years of retirement** while selling shares to fund living expenses permanently depletes your portfolio's capital base.
* *The Solution:* Build a **2-year cash and short-term bond buffer** (cash cushion) so you never have to sell depressed equity assets during a market downturn.
