## The Fisher Equation: Why 12% Minus 6% Does Not Equal 6%

Most casual investors do mental math when thinking about inflation:
*"My mutual fund earns 12%, and inflation is 6%, so my money grows at 6%."*

That quick subtraction sounds intuitive, but mathematically, **it is flat-out wrong.**

Because inflation compounds on top of your investment returns across monthly periods, economists use the **Fisher Equation** to calculate your true real rate of return:

$$1 + r_{\text{nominal}} = (1 + r_{\text{real}}) \times (1 + i_{\text{inflation}})$$

$$r_{\text{real}} = \left[ \frac{1 + r_{\text{nominal}}}{1 + i_{\text{inflation}}} \right] - 1$$

Let's plug in the actual numbers:
* Expected Nominal Return ($r_{\text{nominal}}$) = **12.0%** ($0.12$)
* Consumer Inflation ($i_{\text{inflation}}$) = **6.0%** ($0.06$)

$$r_{\text{real}} = \left[ \frac{1 + 0.12}{1 + 0.06} \right] - 1 = \frac{1.12}{1.06} - 1 \approx \mathbf{5.66\%}$$

```
Mental Shortcut Subtraction:    12.0% - 6.0% = 6.00%
ACTUAL Real Compound Rate:      (1.12 / 1.06) - 1 = 5.66%
```

A difference of **0.34%** might look like split hairs. 

Across a 20-year investment horizon with monthly compounding, that tiny 0.34% compounding gap accounts for a difference of **lakhs of rupees in real purchasing power!**

An advanced **sip calculator free online** doesn't use sloppy subtraction shortcuts; it executes the exact Fisher equation behind the scenes, ensuring your financial planning is grounded in rigorous mathematical truth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Why do basic free SIP calculators give an inaccurate picture of retirement wealth?
Basic calculators assume a completely static environment: zero inflation, zero salary progression, and zero capital gains taxes. 

They output a gross nominal maturity figure that looks impressive on screen, but fails to reflect what that money will actually buy twenty years from now. 

Real-world financial planning requires modeling annual salary step-ups, consumer inflation rates, and capital gains tax deductions.

### 2. How does the Fisher equation calculate real returns in an online SIP calculator?
The Fisher equation determines your real rate of return by dividing your nominal growth rate by your inflation rate:
$$r_{\text{real}} = \left[ \frac{1 + r_{\text{nominal}}}{1 + i_{\text{inflation}}} \right] - 1$$

If your mutual fund portfolio earns a 12% nominal return while inflation averages 6%, your real purchasing power expands at **5.66% per year**, rather than a simple arithmetic subtraction of 6%.
