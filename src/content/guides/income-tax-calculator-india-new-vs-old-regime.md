Navigating Indian income tax has become a strategic decision since the introduction of two parallel taxation frameworks: the **New Tax Regime** (default under Section 115BAC) and the **Old Tax Regime**.

Choosing the correct regime can save you anywhere from ₹20,000 to over ₹1,50,000 annually. In this guide, we break down **how to calculate income tax in India for 2026**, compare slab rates, and demonstrate the exact mathematical breakeven point.

## 1. New vs. Old Tax Regime Slabs for AY 2026-27

### New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC Default):
The New Regime offers lower progressive slab rates but eliminates most exemptions.

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
| :--- | :--- |
| Up to ₹3,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹15,00,000 | 30% |

*Key Benefit:* **Full Section 87A rebate** applies if total taxable income is up to ₹7,00,000. Coupled with the ₹75,000 standard deduction, **salaried individuals earning up to ₹7.75 Lakh pay ₹0 tax**!

### Old Tax Regime:
The Old Regime features fewer, steeper tax brackets but preserves itemized deductions.

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
| :--- | :--- |
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

## 2. Key Deductions: What Is Allowed Where?

| Deduction Type | Section | Old Regime | New Regime |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Deduction (Salaried)** | 16(ia) | ₹50,000 | **₹75,000** |
| **PPF, ELSS, EPF, LIC** | 80C (up to ₹1.5L) | Allowed | **Not Allowed** |
| **Health Insurance Premium** | 80D (up to ₹1L) | Allowed | **Not Allowed** |
| **House Rent Allowance (HRA)** | 10(13A) | Allowed | **Not Allowed** |
| **Home Loan Interest (Self-occupied)** | 24(b) (up to ₹2L) | Allowed | **Not Allowed** |
| **National Pension Scheme (NPS)** | 80CCD(1B) (₹50k) | Allowed | **Not Allowed** |
| **Employer NPS Contribution** | 80CCD(2) (up to 14%) | Allowed | **Allowed** |

## 3. The Breakeven Deduction Formula

How do you determine which regime is better for your specific salary? Use the **Breakeven Deduction Threshold**:

If your total eligible deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + Home Loan Interest) exceed the breakeven threshold for your income band, the **Old Regime** wins. If your deductions fall below the threshold, the **New Regime** wins.

### General Breakeven Guideline:
* **Gross Income ₹10 Lakh:** Breakeven deductions ≈ ₹2,50,000
* **Gross Income ₹15 Lakh:** Breakeven deductions ≈ ₹3,75,000
* **Gross Income ₹20 Lakh:** Breakeven deductions ≈ ₹4,25,000

## 4. Step-by-Step Calculation Comparison

Let's evaluate a salaried professional earning **₹15,00,000 gross annual salary**:
* **Scenario A:** Claiming Standard Deduction only.
* **Scenario B:** Claiming Section 80C (₹1.5L), 80D (₹25k), and Home Loan Interest (₹2L) = ₹3.75 Lakh deductions.

### Under the New Tax Regime:
* Gross Salary: ₹15,00,000
* Standard Deduction: -₹75,000
* Taxable Income: ₹14,25,000
* **Total Tax Payable (with 4% cess): ₹1,27,400**

### Under the Old Tax Regime (Scenario B):
* Gross Salary: ₹15,00,000
* Standard Deduction: -₹50,000
* Chapter VI-A Deductions: -₹3,75,000
* Taxable Income: ₹10,75,000
* **Total Tax Payable (with 4% cess): ₹1,37,800**
* *Verdict:* Even with ₹3.75 Lakh in deductions, the **New Tax Regime saves ₹10,400**!
